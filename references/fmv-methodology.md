# FMV Methodology Notes

How fair market value reviews are actually performed, and the mistakes this tool is
built to avoid. Sources at the bottom.

## What FMV means

**Fair market value** (Stark): the value in arm's-length transactions, consistent with
general market value — the compensation that would result from bona fide bargaining
between well-informed parties who are *not* in a position to generate business for
each other.

CMS removed the old hourly "safe harbor" in Stark II Phase III and replaced it with a
principle: parties may use *"any commercially reasonable methodology that is appropriate
under the circumstances."* There is no prescribed formula.

**Commercial reasonableness is a separate test.** An arrangement can be at FMV and still
fail commercial reasonableness (e.g. paying for a service the organization has no
legitimate need for). This tool addresses FMV positioning only.

## The three approaches

| Approach | Question it answers |
|---|---|
| **Market** | What are comparable providers paid for similar services in similar settings? |
| **Income** | What is the present value of the expected future benefit of the service? |
| **Cost** | What would it cost to replace this service (substitute coverage, locums, employed pro-forma)? |

**This tool performs the market approach only.** A complete FMV determination reconciles
all three against the facts. Guidance also cautions against mechanically averaging or
assigning fixed weights to factors — consider the totality of the circumstances.

## Using survey data correctly

**Reference multiple independent surveys** where sample sizes permit; CMS commentary
calls this prudent practice.

**Know what blending costs you.** Providers who respond to more than one survey are
double-counted in a blend. This tool blends by sample size and discloses that overlap
is not adjusted for.

**Survey populations differ.** Publishers skew toward different settings — group practice
versus academic/health-system — so the right survey depends on the provider being
reviewed, not on which number is most convenient.

**Definitions differ.** Total cash compensation is defined slightly differently by each
publisher. Confirm the components being compared match the survey's definition.

**Data lags.** Surveys are typically published one to two years in arrears. Consider
whether market movement since the survey date is material.

**National over regional.** Survey "regions" bundle dissimilar markets and are defined
differently by each publisher; two cities in one state can land in different regions
across surveys. Use national data and adjust for local factors explicitly.

**Watch sample size.** With a small n, a single respondent can move a percentile materially.

## Percentile myths

**"Under the 75th percentile is FMV."** False. 75% of respondents fall below p75 by
definition; if everyone is paid at p75 the benchmark migrates upward. More importantly,
paying p75 to a provider producing at p25 is a genuine misalignment regardless of label.

**"Above the 90th percentile is never FMV."** Also false. 10% of providers sit at or
above p90 and should be paid accordingly. Stacked arrangements — high production plus
call plus administrative duties — can legitimately aggregate above p90. CMS has stated
it has never been their policy to set percentile parameters.

**The real test is alignment**, plus documentation of the facts and circumstances.

## Ratio pitfalls

**Compensation per wRVU.** Surveys compute each metric independently. A provider at p90
of wRVUs is *not* thereby entitled to p90 compensation-per-wRVU. Market comp-per-wRVU is
relatively flat across quartiles — applying a high-quartile ratio to high production
double-counts productivity and inflates supportable compensation.

High effective comp-per-wRVU often signals a provider on an income guarantee ramping up
a panel (compensation near median, production near p25), not a market outlier.

**Compensation to collections above 100%** is normal for coverage-based specialties
(hospitalists, emergency medicine) and high-Medicaid specialties (pediatrics). It is not
by itself evidence of an FMV problem.

## Apples-to-apples productivity

wRVUs billed by advanced practice providers under a physician's identifier inflate that
physician's apparent production against benchmarks that exclude APP work. Confirm what
is included before comparing.

## Factors beyond the survey table

Survey data alone does not establish FMV. Documented factors that legitimately affect
supportable compensation include: specialty supply and demand; administrative, teaching
and academic duties; call burden; years of experience; credentials and specialized
training; community need and shortage designations; payer mix and reimbursement trends;
the consequences of not securing the service (licensure, accreditation, trauma
designation); historical compensation; and prior declined offers.

## Documentation standard

Write the analysis so a reader unfamiliar with the situation — a regulator, an auditor —
could follow the background, facts, assumptions, analysis and rationale to the same
conclusion. If a figure is derived or interpolated, label it as such.
